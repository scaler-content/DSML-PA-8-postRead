Setting the Scene

🎯 The Business Problem

The marketing lead drops by: "We've got a Rs. 50,000 budget for this quarter's campaign. We have over 2,000 customers — that budget doesn't stretch to everyone. So who do we actually spend it on?"

Blasting the same offer to every customer wastes budget on people who were never going to buy anyway, and under-serves customers who are about to walk away. The answer: customer segmentation using RFM — grouping customers purely by how valuable and at-risk they each are, from nothing but the transaction data already in the database.

🔄 Flip of Last Lecture

Guesstimates taught you to build a number from nothing. CRM Analytics does the opposite: you have thousands of real transactions and the job is to turn them into a marketing strategy.

Customer Segmentation

Segmenting customers helps you focus marketing efforts so you can increase profits and customer satisfaction. Four main lenses are commonly used:

👤 Demographic

Age, gender, income, education level, marital status, occupation.

🧠 Psychographic

Personality, lifestyle, social status, interests, values, beliefs.

🌍 Geographic

Country, region, city, climate, urban vs. rural, population density.

🛒 Behavioral ← RFM lives here

Purchase frequency, order value, category, channel, brand loyalty. This is the lens RFM targets directly.

RFM Framework

RFM derives customer value entirely from transaction history — no survey needed. It captures the three dimensions that best predict future buying behaviour:

R

Recency

How recently did the customer make a purchase? Measured in days, weeks or months depending on your purchase cycle.

Lower = Better
F

Frequency

How often does the customer purchase, typically over the 12 months leading up to their last purchase?

Higher = Better
M

Monetary

How much money does the customer spend? Either total or average over the same 12-month window.

Higher = Better

Q1 · Why does RFM use Recency, Frequency and Monetary value — rather than, say, age or location — to judge customer value?
A Because age and location are impossible to collect
B Because R, F and M are all derived purely from a customer's own transaction history, so they directly measure buying behaviour rather than who the customer is
C Because RFM is required by law for e-commerce companies
D Because Recency, Frequency and Monetary are always equally weighted in every business
✓ Correct! Demographic traits like age or location describe WHO a customer is — they don't tell you whether that customer is currently engaged. R, F and M are computed straight from the transaction log itself — no survey needed — and together they capture exactly the behaviour that predicts future value: did they buy recently, do they buy often, and do they spend a lot when they do?

The RFM Formulas

-- Step 1: Anchor the analysis to the most recent order date Reference Date =MAX('Class Data'!$C2:$C27373) -- Step 2–4: Compute R, F, M at order level Recency_value =H2-G2 // days since last purchase Frequency_value =COUNTIF($F$2:$F$27373, F2) Monetary_value =SUMIF($F$2:$F$27373, F2, $E$2:$E$27373) -- Step 5: Percentile cut-offs — one pair per metric (33rd / 66th) Recency =PERCENTILE.EXC(C3:C2013, 0.33) → K5 =PERCENTILE.EXC(C3:C2013, 0.66) → K6 Frequency =PERCENTILE.EXC(D3:D2013, 0.33) → K12 =PERCENTILE.EXC(D3:D2013, 0.66) → K13 Monetary =PERCENTILE.EXC(E3:E2013, 0.33) → K19 =PERCENTILE.EXC(E3:E2013, 0.66) → K20 -- Scoring: Recency is INVERTED (lower days = better = score 3) Recency Score =IF(C3<=$K$5, 3, IF(C3>$K$6, 1, 2)) Frequency Score =IF(D3<=$K$12, 1, IF(D3>$K$13, 3, 2)) Monetary Score =IF(E3<=$K$19, 1, IF(E3>$K$20, 3, 2)) RFM Score =CONCATENATE(F3,G3,H3)

The Actual Cut-Offs from the Class Data

Those percentile formulas return these thresholds. Roughly a third of the 2,013 customers lands in each band — and notice Recency runs the opposite way to the other two.

⚠️ The Recency Inversion

For Recency, LOW values are BETTER (fewer days since last purchase = more recent). This means the scoring formula runs in the opposite direction from Frequency and Monetary. Miss this inversion and every customer segment downstream comes out backwards.

Q2 · A customer has an RFM score of "313" (Recency=3, Frequency=1, Monetary=3). What does this tell you about them?
A They buy often but haven't purchased in a long time and spend little
B They purchased very recently and spend a lot, but haven't bought often
C They are an average customer across all three dimensions
D The score is invalid because Recency and Monetary can't both be high
✓ Correct! Recency=3 means they bought very recently (best score). Frequency=1 means low order count — they haven't purchased often. Monetary=3 means they've spent a lot. Put together: a recent, high-spending customer with only a few orders. That could be a new customer who started big, or an occasional buyer who just came back — RFM alone can't tell which, because it doesn't capture when the customer first purchased.

Q3 · A customer has RFM score 133 (Recency=1, Frequency=3, Monetary=3). Which segment are they in, and why?
A Champions — because Monetary is at its highest
B Can't Lose Them — high F and M (they used to shop a lot), but the lowest Recency means it's been 259+ days since their last purchase
C About to Sleep — because Recency is at its lowest
D Promising — because Frequency is high
✓ Correct! Frequency=3 and Monetary=3 mean this customer shops very often and spends a lot — a genuinely high-value customer. But Recency=1 means it's been a very long time (259+ days) since their last purchase. That combination — valuable customer going quiet — is exactly "Can't Lose Them": someone the business must not let slip away.

Use Case: RFM at Amazon

By applying RFM segments, Amazon tailors marketing intensity across five tiers — from VIP treatment at the top to re-engagement at the bottom — to maximise customer lifetime value across its entire base:

🏆 High RFM — VIP Treatment

  • Personalised product recommendations from purchase/browsing history
  • Early access to new product releases and limited-time items
  • Invite to Amazon Prime with extra benefits (faster shipping, exclusive content)

💚 Moderate-High — Nurture

  • Tailored recommendations, but fewer promotional emails
  • Loyalty rewards — gift cards, discounts — for continued engagement
  • Early access to some deals to maintain interest

⚠️ Moderate — Encourage

  • Product bundles/kits to encourage additional purchases
  • Time-limited promotions ("spend X, get Y")
  • Re-engagement emails reminding of past shopping experiences

📉 Moderate-Low — Win-Back

  • At risk of going inactive — win-back emails with attractive discounts or special offers
  • Abandoned cart reminders to prompt them to complete the purchase
  • Personalised suggestions from categories they have shown interest in before

🚨 Low RFM — Re-Engage

  • The hardest group, but still has potential
  • Customer surveys to gather feedback on their experience, then feed that back into better suggestions
  • Compelling promotions to entice a return — a next-purchase discount or free shipping above a minimum order

💡 Back to Aditi's Rs. 50,000 Problem

Instead of splitting the budget evenly across 2,000+ customers: spend the bulk on Champions (highest value) and "Can't lose them" (high value, high risk of churn), a moderate amount on Potential Loyalists and New customers, and cheap, automated win-back emails on the Hibernating / At Risk and About to sleep groups. Same budget — dramatically more return — because the spend now follows the segment.
